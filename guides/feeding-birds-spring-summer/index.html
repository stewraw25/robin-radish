Feeding birds in spring and summer

By Robin & Radish · 21 November 2026

The RSPB's current advice is to pause seeds and peanuts from 1 May to 31 October. Small amounts of mealworms, fat balls or suet can still go out, in hanging feeders that you clean weekly.

From 1 May to 31 October, pause seeds and peanuts. That is the RSPB's current advice, set out in its "Feed Seasonally, Feed Safely" guidance dated 30 October 2026. You can still offer small amounts of mealworms, fat balls or suet. Use hanging feeders rather than a bird table or a flat tray, clean them every week, and change the water every day if you put water out.

The RSPB's page is Our latest guidance on feeding garden birds. From 1 November to 30 April you can offer the full range again, including seeds and peanuts. Those winter foods are listed in bird food for winter. Cleaning, and what to do if birds look unwell, is in cleaning bird feeders.

Why the pause

The RSPB says the risk of trichomonosis, a contagious disease that spreads where birds gather, is higher in summer and autumn. That is also when natural food, such as insects and seed, is easier to find. The pause is meant to cut the chance of birds passing the disease on at a busy feeder, not to leave them without food.

You do not have to put anything out in these months. If you stop, the birds that live in the garden do not vanish. If you carry on, keep to the foods below, in small amounts, and keep the feeder clean. Warm, wet weather spoils food faster than a frost does.

What you can still put out

Mealworms, fat balls or suet, in small amounts, can be offered all year.

Soak dried mealworms before you put them out. The RSPB asks that mealworms are eaten or cleared away by dusk the same day, especially if any are scattered on the ground, so leftovers are not taken by hedgehogs. A hanging mealworm feeder is a better place for them than a tray.

Hang fat balls or suet in a cage or a hanging feeder. The RSPB says suet can stay out for up to seven days. If a warm spell makes the fat soft enough to smear, take it down. Soft fat on feathers is a problem. A mild week in April or May is enough.

Do not put out seeds, seed mixes, sunflower hearts, nyjer or peanuts between 1 May and 31 October. That includes whole peanuts in a mesh feeder. The RSPB's advice is to pause those foods even if you clean the feeder every day.

How to offer it

The RSPB asks you to choose hanging feeders over flat feeders such as bird tables, window feeders and trays, because there is a higher risk of disease spreading on a flat surface, where contaminated food can collect. Hang the feeder where rain is less likely to soak the food, and not under a tree or a perch where droppings fall.

Clean the feeder every week, and move it to a different spot every week so droppings and spilled food do not build up on one patch of ground. Sweep or rake what is underneath. Put out only what will be eaten while it is fresh.

A shallow dish of water still helps in a dry May, but only put water out if you can change it every day. The trichomonosis parasite can survive in water. Water for garden birds covers the dish.

What not to put out

Do not put out salted, dry-roasted or flavoured nuts, mouldy or damp seed, milk, desiccated coconut, or cooked food seasoned the way you would eat it. Cooking fat that has held salt is not a suet cake.

Bread is not one of the foods the RSPB currently lists on its feeding pages. It should not be used as a summer filler, and it should not be the thing you leave on a table.

Windfall apples on the ground are still useful for blackbirds. That is fruit, not scraps from a plate. Do not add leftovers.

If you go away, empty the feeder and the bath before you leave, or ask someone to do the same small top-up you do. A feeder of rotting food for a fortnight is worse than no feeder. Birds used the garden before the holiday. They will use it when you are back, provided you start again with clean equipment.

Where to buy the food

Buy a small amount in warm weather, because food spoils faster. Do not buy seed or peanuts for this period, and do not add a bird table to get through the summer.

Common questions

Should I stop feeding in April?

April is still inside the RSPB's 1 November to 30 April window, so seeds and peanuts can stay out until the end of the month if they are eaten quickly and the feeder is kept clean. The pause on seeds and peanuts starts on 1 May. Stopping earlier is a reasonable choice. Stopping and leaving old seed in the tube is not.

Can I feed mealworms while birds are nesting?

Yes, in small amounts, as a supplement. Soak dried mealworms, use a hanging feeder if you can, and clear what is left by dusk. The RSPB notes that animal protein such as mealworms can be offered year-round. The chicks of tits and robins still need the insects their parents catch. Mealworms are not a complete replacement for that.

Can I keep putting seed out if I clean the feeder every day?

No. The RSPB's advice is to pause seeds and peanuts from 1 May to 31 October even if you clean every day. Weekly cleaning still matters for the mealworms and suet you do put out, and for the water dish.

What if finches at the feeder look fluffed and slow?

Take the feeders and the bath down, clean them, clear spilled food, and store them away from the garden. The RSPB's advice, if you see a sick bird, is to pause feeding for a month, and only start again if no more sick birds are seen. The cleaning guide sets out the steps. That is general garden practice, not a diagnosis.
