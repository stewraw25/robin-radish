Herbs for a small space

By Robin & Radish · 17 November 2026

A short list of herbs that earn a UK windowsill or a metre of bed, and which ones need a pot of their own.

A small space can grow herbs. It cannot grow a dozen of them well. Pick the ones you will cut for the kitchen, give the thirsty ones a pot you can reach, and let the woody ones have sun and a dry neck. Pot depth, compost and watering for vegetables are in growing in containers. The months are in the planner.

A windowsill

Basil is the summer windowsill herb. Sow it indoors and do not plant it out until June. Cold nights finish it. Pinch the tips. Water the compost and do not leave the pot standing in a saucer.

Parsley is slower to germinate and more useful over a long season. A pot by the door often does better than a sill above a radiator. Chives come back each year from a small pot. Cut leaves and leave some.

Coriander runs to seed in the warm. Sow a pinch, use it, and sow again. One large sowing bolts together.

Outside, in a metre

Thyme and rosemary want sun and sharp drainage. Wet winter compost kills them more often than frost does. A pot against the house is safer than a heavy clay bed. Pick lightly in the first year.

Mint is the one that must have its own pot. In a shared trough it runs through everything else. Sink the pot in the ground if you want it to look planted, and check that roots are not escaping the bottom.

A single outdoor row can be chives, parsley and thyme, with mint beside them in a pot. That is enough for most kitchens. Adding every packet in the shop in March gives you crowded, thin plants.

How to cut them

Take tips and outer leaves. Do not shear a young plant to the soil because a recipe needs a handful. Basil and mint grow back from a cut above a pair of leaves. Rosemary and thyme resent being cut into old bare wood. Leave some leaf on the plant.

The herb pages have the sowing months: basil stays indoors until June, parsley can be sown outdoors from spring, rosemary is usually started as a small plant rather than seed.

Seed and plants

Common questions

Can I grow herbs on a balcony that only gets morning sun?

Parsley, chives and mint will cope with half a day of light. Basil, rosemary and thyme want more sun than that. If the balcony is windy, the pots dry out even when the light is poor. Feel the compost.

Should every herb be in one long trough?

No. Mint needs a pot of its own. Rosemary and thyme like a gritty compost. Basil likes more water and more feed than thyme does. Three pots beat one crowded trough.

What survives a UK winter in a pot?

Chives die back and return. Thyme and an established rosemary can stay out if the compost drains and the pot is not waterlogged. Basil will not. Parsley sometimes stands and is not reliable for a second summer. Sow it again.
