Planning a vegetable plot for 2027

By Robin & Radish · 21 November 2026

A winter plan for a UK vegetable plot in 2027: beds, seed to order, and what not to sow yet.

Planning is the useful December job. The soil is often unfit to dig, and most seed should stay in the packet. A sketch and a seed list will do more for 2027 than a row sown into cold mud.

This is not a forecast, and it is not a new set of dates. The months are the ones already on the sowing planner and the 2027 sowing calendar. If you are opening a first bed, start with starting a vegetable patch. If the beds exist, this page is about what to put in them.

What is already in the ground

Walk the plot when the soil is firm enough, or look from the path. Note what is standing: leeks, parsnips, Brussels sprouts, kale and swede are the winter harvests on this site. Garlic and autumn broad beans occupy a row until summer. Do not plan a March sowing on top of them.

Mark roots before the tops vanish. A stick at each end of a parsnip row is enough.

A sketch, not a redesign

Draw the beds you actually have, including the shady end and the place that stays wet. Put tall crops where they will not shade a short one all afternoon. Leave a path. If you walk on the soil you grow in, it packs hard, especially on clay.

You do not need a four-bed system before you own four beds. The useful habit is in crop rotation: avoid putting cabbage, kale, sprouts and other brassicas in the exact same spot every year if you have room to move them. Garlic and onions prefer not to follow each other in a bed that has had white rot.

No-dig beds and raised beds are ways to manage the soil you have. Neither one creates a sowing month that the planner does not list.

What to order

Order seed for crops you will pick and eat, not a packet of everything in the catalogue. A small plot can run on a short list: a salad, a root, a bean, something for winter, and a herb you use. The month pages show when each one is sown in the south and in the north.

South here means southern England, the Midlands and much of Wales. North means northern England and Scotland. Those are ranges, not a promise that your garden matches the postcode.

Write the month next to each packet when it arrives. Seed sown in the wrong month is the usual waste. What to sow in winter is the list of what can happen before spring, and what should wait.

Check dates on old packets. Many vegetable seeds still germinate the next year if they were kept dry and cool. Onion seed loses strength sooner than peas or beans. If you are unsure, sow a few on a damp paper towel indoors before you depend on the packet. That test does not mean the crop belongs in a December bed.

What to leave until the days lengthen

Do not sow tomatoes, courgettes, French beans, runner beans, cucumbers or sweetcorn in December or January. They need more light than a winter windowsill gives them. On this site, tomatoes start indoors from February in the south and March in the north.

The gap after winter crops finish, and before summer ones are ready, is the hungry gap. Planning a row of spring cabbage or purple sprouting is how that gap gets smaller. Those crops were sown last year. You cannot add them in January and eat them in May.

Compost can be spread on a bed you will not be walking on. Digging wet soil smears it. If the spade comes up shiny, stop.

Common questions

When should I buy seed for 2027?

Winter is a practical time, because you can match the packets to the planner before the sowing months begin. Buying in a rush in April is how a plot fills up with crops that all need the same week. There is no week in which the seed "must" be ordered.

Do I need a greenhouse before I plan?

No. A bright windowsill covers the few sowings that start indoors, if you do not start them too early. Outdoor months on the planner do not require a greenhouse. A cold frame helps with sowing under cover, and you can garden without one.

Can I plan the same bed as last year?

Yes, if it grew well and you can move the brassicas. Note what failed. A row that sat in water all winter is a drainage problem, not a variety problem. Fix the path and the wet end before you add more crops.
