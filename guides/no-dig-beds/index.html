No-dig beds for vegetables

By Robin & Radish · 17 November 2026

How a no-dig vegetable bed works in a UK garden: compost on the surface, cardboard on a new plot, and what still has to be done by hand.

No-dig means you stop turning the bed over each year. Compost goes on the surface, worms take it down, and you sow or plant into that layer. It is a way of managing a home plot. It is not a guarantee of a heavier crop, and it does not replace watering, weeding or netting.

If you are still marking out the plot, read starting a vegetable patch first. A framed bed is covered in raised beds. Spacing for each crop stays on its own page, for example carrots and potatoes.

A new bed

On lawn or weedy ground, lay plain cardboard, overlap the edges, and wet it. Cover it with compost deep enough to sow into, often around 10 to 15 centimetres for a first season. The cardboard rots. Roots of established perennial weeds, such as bindweed and couch, can come through. Pull those when you see them. Cardboard does not finish that job on its own.

Do not walk on the bed. Paths take the traffic. In a wet UK winter, especially on clay, standing on the soil undoes the point of leaving it unturned.

Each year after that

Spread a layer of garden compost or a bought soil improver on the surface in autumn or winter, when you can get it. You do not have to dig it in. In spring, draw a drill in the surface and sow, or pull aside a little compost and set a plant.

Leave the paths where they are. A bed that stays in the same place is easier to mulch, and you are not turning the weeds on the path into the crop.

Fresh manure is a poor surface for carrots and parsnips. It makes roots fork. Use a settled compost for those rows, and read the carrot and parsnip notes before you sow them.

What no-dig does not fix

Slugs like a damp mulch. Seedlings of lettuce, beans and brassicas still need watching. A mulch is not a slug treatment.

Stones still fork carrots. If the ground underneath is rubble, a deeper bed or a container is the honest answer. See growing in containers for pots.

A closed cloche or a late frost is a separate problem. No-dig does not warm a bed the way a cover does. Sowing under cover is the note for that.

The sowing planner is unchanged by the method. The months you sow broad beans or kale are the same. What changes is that you are not digging the row first.

Compost to start a bed

A first covering takes more compost than a yearly mulch. You can search Amazon for garden compost (opens in a new tab). That is a search, not a particular bag. A local green-waste heap or a neighbour's surplus is often cheaper than postage.

Common questions

Do I have to dig the bed once to start?

On reasonable soil, no. Cardboard and compost over the surface is the usual start. If a tree root or a buried path is in the way, move that. You are not trying to invert the whole plot.

Can I grow potatoes without digging?

Yes. Set the tubers on the surface or in a shallow hollow and cover them with compost, then add more as the stems grow. They still need that cover, or the new potatoes go green. The early potato page has the planting months.

Will weeds disappear?

Annual weed seedlings still appear, and they are easy to hoe while they are small. Perennial weeds come back until you have pulled the roots. A mulch makes them easier to see. It does not delete them.
