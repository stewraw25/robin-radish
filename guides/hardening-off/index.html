Hardening off seedlings

By Robin & Radish · 17 November 2026

How to move UK seedlings from a windowsill or greenhouse outside without losing them to wind or a cold night.

A plant raised indoors has soft growth and no experience of wind. Put it straight into the garden on a bright day and the leaves scorch or the stem folds. Hardening off is the week or so you spend getting it used to the outdoors before it stays out.

This is for crops you sow under cover: tomatoes, chillies, courgettes, French beans, runner beans and the brassicas you started in pots, such as calabrese. The month still has to be right. Hardening off does not make a courgette frost-proof. Check the sowing calendar and the planner for when planting out is on the list.

A simple routine

Pick a spell that is not forecast to freeze at night.

  • Day one: stand the pots outside in a sheltered spot for a couple of hours, out of full sun and strong wind, then bring them in.
  • Over the next days: leave them out longer, and let them see more sun.
  • After several days: leave them out overnight if the night is mild, still somewhere you can cover them.
  • Then plant them, and water them in.

If a cold night is forecast in the middle of this, bring them in or cover them. There is no prize for finishing the routine on a date you wrote in a diary.

A cold frame makes this easier because you open the lid a little more each day. A windowsill works too. You just carry the tray.

What still kills them

Wind. A plant that has never moved will snap at the stem in a gust. Start them against a wall, not on an open table.

Sun. Glass and a south window are hot, but outdoor sun on a soft leaf is different. A first afternoon in full sun bleaches the leaves.

A late frost. Sowing under cover gets the plants started. It does not change the last frost. Tender crops on this site are planted from May in the south and June in the north. That is a range, not a promise about your garden.

Dry compost. A pot in the wind dries faster than it did on the sill. Feel the compost. Do not water on a timetable.

Common questions

How long does hardening off take?

Several days, often about a week, longer if the weather turns. If the plants wilt every time they go out, you are going too fast. Bring them in and try a shorter spell the next day.

Can I skip it if the days are warm?

A warm afternoon does not mean the plant is ready, and it does not mean the night will stay warm. The soft growth is the problem, not only the temperature. Give them the staged days. If you are late and a frost is not forecast, a couple of sheltered days outside is still better than none.
