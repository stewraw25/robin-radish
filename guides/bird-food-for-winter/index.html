Bird food for winter

By Robin & Radish · 16 November 2026

Plain notes on sunflower hearts, nyjer, suet, mealworms and mixed seed for UK garden birds in winter, plus water and foods to avoid.

Winter is when a garden feeder earns its keep. Days are short, the ground freezes, and insects are scarce. You do not have to feed birds, and there is no single food that every species needs. A small selection, kept fresh, covers most of the birds that use a UK garden.

If you would rather answer a few questions than read the whole list, try the bird food quiz. Feeder types, without prices or rankings, are in the bird feeder buying guide. What changes once winter is over is in feeding birds in spring and summer. The notes below are the same foods, with the reasons written out. Species pages: robin, blue tit, goldfinch and blackbird.

Five ordinary foods

Sunflower hearts are the kernel with the shell removed. Tits, finches, sparrows and many other birds eat them. Husks do not pile up under the feeder. Keep the seed dry, and put out what will be eaten before it turns damp.

Nyjer is a very small, oil-rich seed. Goldfinches and siskins take it. It needs a feeder with tiny holes. In a normal seed feeder it pours onto the ground. Other birds usually ignore it, which is useful if pigeons empty everything else.

Suet is sold as blocks, pellets or fat balls. Tits, starlings and woodpeckers take it in cold weather. Hang it in a cage. If a mild spell makes it soft enough to smear, take it down. Fat on feathers is a problem, not a help.

Mealworms, dried or live, are the food robins and blackbirds often take first. Offer them on a ground tray or bird table, not only in a hanging tube. Soak dried mealworms in water before you put them out so they are easier to eat and hold a little moisture.

Mixed seed is a blend, and the blend matters. A bag that is mostly wheat or maize is often kicked to the ground and can feed pigeons more than tits. Look for a mix with a clear share of sunflower and millet. Store it dry and put out a day or two of food, not a heap that sits in the rain.

Water, amounts and squirrels

A shallow dish of water matters as much as seed when everything is frozen. Break the ice in the morning. You do not need a deep bath. Change the water when it is dirty. Water for garden birds is the longer note, and feeder hygiene covers the cleaning.

There is no fixed scoop that suits every garden. Put out what the birds finish while it is still fresh. If food is still there at dusk and it has rained, you put out too much. Top up in the morning. Birds have a long night ahead of them.

Squirrels learn feeders. A baffle on a pole, a cage around the seed, or siting the feeder away from a jumping-off fence can slow them down. None of these stop every squirrel. Nyjer is less interesting to many of them than hearts or suet.

What to leave out of the feeder

Do not put out salted, dry-roasted or flavoured nuts, mouldy seed, desiccated coconut, milk, or cooked food seasoned the way you would eat it. Whole peanuts belong in a rigid mesh feeder so birds chip pieces off, rather than loose on a table where a bird can take a whole nut.

Bread is a poor staple. If you put out a small amount of stale crumbs, it should not be the only food, and it should not be left to go sour.

Apples on the ground are useful for blackbirds and for winter thrushes such as redwings and fieldfares. A windfall is a different thing from a feeder full of scraps.

Common questions

Do I have to feed birds all year?

No. Winter is when natural food is harder to find. If you do feed in summer as well, keep portions small and the feeder clean, and be careful with whole peanuts once birds are nesting. Stopping for a holiday will not empty the garden of birds, as long as you are not the only food in a hard freeze and you start again when you are back.

Which food should I buy if I only want one?

Sunflower hearts, in a hanging feeder or on a tray, suit the widest range of common garden birds. Add mealworms on a tray if a robin is the bird you most want to see. Add nyjer only if goldfinches visit and you have the right feeder.

Can I feed birds if I have a cat?

Yes, with some thought about where the food sits. Give birds a clear view and a shrub to escape into, and do not put a low tray in long grass beside a fence a cat can hide behind. A feeder on a stand, a little way into the open, is easier to watch.
