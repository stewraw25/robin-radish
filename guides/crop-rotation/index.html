Crop rotation for a small plot

By Robin & Radish · 17 November 2026

A practical crop rotation for a UK bed that is too small for a textbook four-year plan, using the plant families on this site.

Rotation means you do not grow the same family in the same patch year after year. Some problems stay in the soil. White rot on onions can persist for years. Clubroot bothers cabbage, kale and sprouts. Moving the family does not sterilise the bed. It stops you making a bad patch worse.

A small garden will not match a four-bed allotment plan. One bed, or two, is enough for a simpler rule: do not put the same family in the same spot two years running, and do not follow potatoes with tomatoes.

The planner is the month list. This page is only about where the crop sits next year. No-dig beds still need the same movement. Compost on the surface does not remove clubroot.

The families on this site

Keep these together when you are deciding what swaps with what.

  • Legumes: broad beans, peas, French beans, runner beans. They are a useful crop to put where a hungry one grew. They are not a fertiliser you can measure from the kitchen.
  • Brassicas: kale, summer cabbage, Brussels sprouts, calabrese, purple sprouting broccoli. Turnips and swede are brassicas too, even though you eat the root. Clubroot does not care which part you cook.
  • Alliums: leeks, onions from seed, onion sets, garlic, spring onions.
  • Potato family: early potatoes, maincrop potatoes, tomatoes and chillies. Treat them as one group. Tomato after potato is not a rotation.
  • Cucurbits: courgettes and cucumbers.
  • Roots: carrots, parsnips, beetroot. Radishes are a quick brassica relative. Do not use a radish row as proof that you rotated away from cabbage if clubroot is in the bed.

Lettuce, rocket, spinach, chard and sweetcorn sit outside those groups. They are useful fillers between the families you are trying to move. Herbs in pots are not in the rotation. Mint stays in its pot. See herbs for a small space.

A two-bed version

Split the vegetable ground into two halves, even if they are small.

One year, put potatoes or tomatoes, and the alliums, on one half. Put brassicas and legumes on the other. Swap the halves the next year. Roots and salads fill the gaps. Write down what grew where. A note on the phone is enough. Memory is how onions end up in the white-rot patch again.

If you only have one raised bed, grow two families in it, split left and right, and swap next spring. You will not achieve a four-year gap. You will avoid the worst habit, which is cabbage in the same corner forever.

Do not rotate onto ground you have just filled with fresh manure if the next crop is carrots or parsnips. They fork. The carrot page says the same thing.

Common questions

Do I have to rotate herbs and lettuce?

Lettuce can follow most crops, and a pot of herbs is not part of the bed rotation. The families that repay the effort are brassicas, alliums, and potatoes with tomatoes.

I only have three bags on a patio. Does this apply?

Only in the sense that you should not use the same compost for tomatoes for years if it grew a sick crop. Fresh compost in a pot is already a move. The bed rules are for soil that stays in the ground.

Can beans go anywhere?

They can follow the crops above. Still move them if the row had a bad soil disease. "Legumes can follow" is not a reason to replant into a bed you know is sick.
