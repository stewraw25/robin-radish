Nest boxes for garden birds

By Robin & Radish · 17 November 2026

Which UK garden birds use a hole box, which need an open front, and where to put a box so it has a chance of being used.

A nest box is an offer. Many are never used. The birds that nest in holes will use one if the hole, the height and the cover are right, and if the garden already has food and somewhere to hide. A box on a bare fence in full sun is often ignored.

Put boxes up in autumn or winter so they are in place before nesting. Once a bird is going in and out, leave the box alone. Do not open it for a look, and do not cut back the ivy in April because it looks untidy.

Species notes: blue tit, great tit, house sparrow, starling and robin.

Hole size

The hole is how you choose the bird.

  • About 25 mm suits blue tits and coal tits, and keeps larger birds out.
  • About 28 mm lets great tits in. Blue tits can still use it.
  • About 32 mm is the house sparrow hole. Sparrows often prefer a row of boxes, or a terrace, under the eaves.
  • About 45 mm suits starlings. The box is larger, and they are noisy tenants.

Do not add a perch. It gives a predator a place to sit. A metal plate around the hole slows squirrels and woodpeckers chewing the entrance wider. It is optional.

Face the box somewhere between north and east if you can, out of the strongest sun and the wettest wind. About two metres up is the usual height for tits. Fix it so it does not flap. A box that faces south and bakes is a poor site in a warm May.

Clean the box out in autumn, when you are sure it is empty. Tip out the old nest. Do not use a strong chemical in it.

Birds that do not want that box

Robins use an open-fronted box, low, hidden in ivy or a shrub, not a small hole. Long-tailed tits build a domed nest in a bush. A hole box will not attract them. Leave the shrub uncut.

Wrens tuck a domed nest into a hollow, thick cover, or sometimes a box used on its side and well hidden. The useful thing is the cover, not a tit box on a pole.

Great spotted woodpeckers dig their own hole in dead wood. They do not use a standard box. A standing dead trunk, where it cannot fall on anyone, is the practical help.

Dunnocks, blackbirds and goldfinches nest in hedges and shrubs. The help is to leave the hedge uncut through spring and summer.

Where to buy a box

A search on Amazon for a bird nest box (opens in a new tab) is a search, not a particular model or a ranking. Check the hole size and that you can open the box to clean it. A local timber offcut is often just as useful.

Common questions

The box has been up for a year and nothing has used it.

That is common. Check the hole size, the sun, and whether a cat can sit on a nearby fence and watch the entrance. Move it in autumn if the site is wrong. Dense shrubs will be used with no box at all.

Can I look inside while they are nesting?

No. Disturbing an active nest can make the adults leave, and opening the box is how eggs get chilled. Watch from a distance. Clean it after the season, in autumn.

Will a box on its own bring birds to the garden?

Only if those birds are already nearby. Food, water and a shrub matter more than the box. The quiz and the water guide cover those. A box is the nesting part, not the whole garden.
