Sow little and often

By Robin & Radish · 17 November 2026

How to sow short rows of UK salads and quick crops every few weeks so they do not all finish on the same day.

One long row of lettuce, sown on a single afternoon, is ready all at once and then bolts. A short row every two or three weeks gives you leaves over a longer stretch, and it wastes less seed. That is successional sowing. It is not a special technique. It is a smaller sowing, repeated.

It suits crops that grow quickly and do not like sitting: lettuce, rocket, radishes, spinach, spring onions, coriander and small turnips. Calabrese is slower, but a second sowing a few weeks after the first spreads the heads.

How to do it

Sow a row you will actually eat before the next one is ready. On a small bed that might be a metre, or a single pot. Write the date on a label. When that row has germinated and you can see it will live, sow the next one.

Stop when the planner says the outdoor sowing window is closing. A July sowing of radish is useful. A November sowing of the same radish, outdoors, is not on the list. The sowing calendar shows the months. It does not split them into weeks.

In hot weather, sow lettuce and rocket where they get a little shade, and water the drill before you sow. Dry soil is why a summer sowing fails, not the idea of sowing again.

What not to repeat

Do not succession-sow crops that occupy the ground for months. Purple sprouting broccoli, Brussels sprouts, leeks and parsnips need one well-timed sowing and a long season. A second row of sprouts in July will not catch up.

Do not sow the next row if you have not thinned the last one. A thick row of radishes, all left in, is how you get a tangle of leaves and no roots.

Common questions

How often is “little and often”?

Every two or three weeks through the sowing months is a workable rhythm for salads. If the weather is cold and the first row has not germinated, wait. Sowing into mud because the calendar says the month has started does not help.

Can I do this in pots?

Yes. A pot of rocket or lettuce, sown again when you have eaten the last one, is the same idea. See growing in containers. A pot dries out faster, so the summer sowings need more attention than the spring ones.
