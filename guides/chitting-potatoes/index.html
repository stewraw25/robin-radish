How to chit seed potatoes

By Robin & Radish · 24 November 2026

When to buy seed potatoes in the UK, how to chit them in a frost-free place, and when to plant early and maincrop types.

Chitting is the winter job of standing seed potatoes in the light so they grow short, sturdy sprouts before you plant them. It is not compulsory, and it is not a way to start potatoes in the dark of a cupboard. The useful window in most UK houses is January and February, before the planting months on the early potatoes and maincrop potatoes pages.

Buy tubers sold as seed potatoes, not a leftover bag from the kitchen. A supermarket potato may be a variety that stores rather than sprouts evenly, and it can carry disease into the bed. Seed potatoes sold for planting are the ones meant for this job.

When to buy

Garden centres and seed shops usually put seed potatoes out from mid-winter. Ordering in January is early enough for most gardens, and still leaves time to chit before planting. There is no prize for buying in November and watching the tubers shrivel in a warm room.

The February month page already mentions chitting. The sowing planner and the crop pages have the planting months, which are later than the chitting months. In the south, earlies are planted from March. In the north they start later. A late, cold spring is a reason to wait, not a reason to plant sprouted tubers into frozen ground.

You can search Amazon for seed potatoes (opens in a new tab) or look at Thompson & Morgan (opens in a new tab). Those are a search and a shop, not a particular variety or a ranking, and this page has no prices. Thompson & Morgan says it cannot ship seeds or plants to Northern Ireland. Check that you are buying seed potatoes, not eating potatoes.

How to chit

Stand each tuber in a tray, an egg box, or a shallow box, with the end that has the most eyes facing up. Put the tray in a cool, frost-free place with good light: a bright porch, a spare room that is not heated hard, or a windowsill that does not freeze at night. The first frost note explains why a garage that drops below freezing is a poor choice.

What you want is short, green or purple sprouts, a centimetre or two long and firm. What you do not want is long, pale, brittle sprouts from a dark, warm cupboard. Those snap off when you plant. If a tuber has already made long white sprouts in the bag, rub the weakest off and put it in the light so the rest stay short.

Turn the tray now and then so the sprouts do not lean hard to one window. Do not water the tubers. They are not in compost yet. A rotten tuber should come out of the tray so it does not spoil its neighbours.

A few weeks in the light is enough for most earlies. Maincrops can have a shorter chit, or go in with only the eyes just breaking, if you are short of space on the sill. The difference is a matter of how you use the weeks, not a fixed number this page can promise for every house.

When to plant

Plant when the soil can be worked and the worst of the hard frosts has passed, using the months on the crop pages. Set the tubers in a trench, sprouts up, and cover them. As the shoots come through, earth up the row so a late frost is less likely to blacken them. A late frost can still scorch unsheltered shoots; keep fleece or soil ready, as the frost guide says.

If planting is delayed, keep the tray cool and light so the sprouts do not race. Do not plant into a waterlogged trench just because the chits look ready.

Once the plants are up, the blight guide is the later-summer note, more often a worry for maincrops than for earlies.

Common questions

Can I plant potatoes without chitting them?

Yes. Planting a firm seed potato with unsprouted eyes still works. Chitting is a head start, especially for earlies, not a rule. Do not delay planting in good weather just because the sprouts are short.

Can I chit supermarket potatoes?

Use seed potatoes sold for planting. A potato from the kitchen may be treated to stay dormant, may be a poor variety for your garden, and may bring disease. It is not a reliable substitute.
