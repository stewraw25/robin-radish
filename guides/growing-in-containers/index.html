Growing vegetables in containers

By Robin & Radish · 17 November 2026

Which vegetables and herbs are worth growing in pots in the UK, how deep the pot needs to be, and why watering matters more than the compost brand.

A pot, a sack or a window box will grow food if it holds enough compost, drains, and is watered. It will not match an open bed for bulk crops such as maincrop potatoes or a row of Brussels sprouts. Use it for the plants that earn a small space.

The month you sow does not change because the plant is in a pot. Check the sowing planner and the crop page. A pot on a patio can be a little warmer than open ground, and it can also freeze harder in winter.

What fits

Leaf and quick crops. Lettuce, radishes, spring onions, spinach and chard are the reliable ones. Sow little and often. A 15 to 20 centimetre depth is enough for leaves and radishes.

Herbs. Basil wants a warm windowsill. Parsley, chives and coriander sit by the door. Mint should be in a pot even if you have a bed, because it runs. Rosemary and thyme need drainage more than rich compost. Wet winter compost kills them.

Fruiting plants. One tomato in a large pot, one courgette in a larger one, or a dwarf French bean. Cucumbers are happier in a greenhouse or frame than on a windy balcony. See sowing under cover. Early potatoes will crop in a sack or a deep pot if you earth them up. They are a small crop, not a winter store.

Carrots need a deep pot and a fine compost, or the roots fork. Short varieties are the ones sold for containers. The carrot page covers carrot fly, which still finds a patio pot.

Compost, water and winter

Use a potting compost, not soil dug out of the garden. Garden soil packs down in a pot and drains badly. The bag does not need to be a special "vegetable" blend. It needs to be fresh enough that it has not dried into a brick, and the pot needs holes in the base.

Water is the job that decides the crop. A pot dries on a mild, windy day even when the lawn is damp. Feel the compost. Fruiting plants that dry out drop flowers. A saucer left full of water rots basil and other herbs. Empty it.

In summer, a fruiting plant in a pot uses up what was in the compost. A general liquid feed, used as the label says, is the usual top-up. Leaves and radishes often finish before that matters.

Pots freeze through. A plant that would survive in the ground can die in a pot in a hard UK frost. Move rosemary and bay-sized herbs against the house, or wrap the pot. Do not assume a balcony is as mild as the soil two metres away.

A full pot of wet compost is heavy. On a balcony, that weight is the thing to check before you line the railing with sacks of potatoes.

Pots and compost

You can search Amazon for plant pots (opens in a new tab), garden compost (opens in a new tab) or grow bags (opens in a new tab). Those are searches, not a particular pot or bag, and not a claim that one is better than another. A used pot, washed out, is fine.

Common questions

Can I grow vegetables in a window box?

Leaves, radishes, spring onions and herbs, yes, if the box is at least 15 centimetres deep and you can water it. Tomatoes and courgettes are too large and too thirsty for a shallow box.

Do container plants need feeding?

Leaf crops in fresh compost often do not. Tomatoes, courgettes and cucumbers usually do once they are flowering, because the pot is a closed amount of compost. Follow the feed you have. More is not better.

Why did the seedlings collapse?

Often they were sown too thickly and kept wet and airless, which is damping off. Sometimes the pot dried out between waterings. Sow thinner, and do not leave a pot sitting in water. The sowing under cover guide covers the same problem on a windowsill.
