UK sowing calendar

What to sow in March

March is when a lot of UK sowing begins, especially in the south. Hardy vegetables can go outdoors if the soil has dried enough to rake. Tender crops such as tomatoes stay indoors. Frost is still ordinary, and more so in the north, so nothing soft is planted out. If the soil sticks to your boots, wait.

South means southern England, the Midlands and much of Wales. North means northern England and Scotland. Frost, soil and a cold spring move the dates. These are ranges, not a forecast for one garden.

In the south, 30 crops have a sowing, planting or harvest job this month. In the north, 20. The lists are the same crops as the sowing planner.

Garden birds this month

Tits, robins, blackbirds, house sparrows and dunnocks are still using food you put out, and they are also singing and looking at nest sites. Natural food is increasing. Keep portions small and fresh. A cold snap sends them back to the feeder in numbers.

Common questions

Can tender vegetables be planted out in March?

No. Tomatoes, courgettes, cucumbers, French beans and runner beans are still killed by frost. Sow them under cover, or keep plants you already have under cover.

The soil is still wet. Should I sow anyway?

No. Seed in cold, waterlogged soil rots. Rake a handful. If it smears rather than crumbles, wait, even if the month on this page has started.

Which birds are at a UK feeder in March?

Blue tits, great tits, robins, blackbirds, house sparrows and dunnocks are the usual group. Clean the feeder. Birds are pairing, and a dirty perch is a poor place for them to meet.
