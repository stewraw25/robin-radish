Ireland

Sowing times in Ireland

How the UK south and north ranges on this site compare with a garden in Ireland. Ranges, not a forecast, and not a new month for each crop.

South means southern England, the Midlands and much of Wales. North means northern England and Scotland. Frost, soil and a cold spring move the dates. These are ranges, not a forecast for one garden.

Open the sowing planner and choose North. That is the cautious start for most of Ireland: closer to the cooler, wetter end of the UK ranges than to the south-east of England. The south list is southern England, the Midlands and much of Wales. It is not a description of the south of Ireland. The Ireland hub says why, including the Met Éireann and Teagasc notes this reading rests on.

Where the two lists differ, follow the north months. Where they are the same, that shared month is the range for both. Do not invent an earlier or later week inside the month. The list was never split into early, mid and late.

A wet, cold soil is a reason to wait. Teagasc notes that waterlogged ground is slow to warm, and that soils are generally coldest in January and February. An early month on the UK south list is not a reason to sow into mud. Under cover, on a bright windowsill or in a greenhouse, is a different job from sowing outdoors. The crop page says which of those the UK lists include.

The 2027 sowing calendar uses the same crop months. The bank holidays printed on the sheets are England and Wales. They are not Irish public holidays. Use the sheets for the crop ranges, not for a day off.

Month pages and the crop pages stay in the UK voice. Read the north column, then the note on the crop for spacing and what usually goes wrong. Nothing on those pages has been rewritten as an Irish date.

For first and last frost, and for fleece or bringing pots under cover, see first frost dates and protecting plants. That guide is written for UK gardens and does not publish a date for Irish towns. Use Met Éireann and your own garden notes, as this page already says, rather than copying a UK table.

Common questions

Can I move every UK date by two weeks for Ireland?

No. This site does not use a fixed shift in Britain, and it does not use one in Ireland. The north list is already a range of months. Frost, wet soil and a cold spring move a garden inside that range. They do not add a set number of days.

What if the garden is on the south or east coast?

It can be earlier than an inland or western garden, especially in a mild spring. Met Éireann’s climate figures show coastal stations have far fewer nights below freezing than inland stations. That is not a licence to copy the UK south list for every crop. Sow when the soil is workable, and keep the north list as the cautious start.
