Growing food

Vegetables and herbs

One page for each crop in the planner. Months come from the shared list. Spacing and problems are general UK garden practice, not a forecast for your soil.

Vegetables

  • Broad beans

    Hardy types can be sown in autumn in milder gardens, or in pots under cover. In the north, spring sowing is the more reliable outdoor crop.

  • Peas

    Autumn sowing is for hardy types in a mild garden, ideally under a cloche. Give them something to climb.

  • French beans

    Frost tender. Wait until the nights have warmed before sowing or planting outside.

  • Runner beans

    Put the supports in before you sow or plant. Flowers set more reliably if the roots are not dry.

  • Carrots

    Sow thinly in fine soil and thin the seedlings. Cover the row to keep carrot fly off.

  • Beetroot

    Each seed cluster can give several seedlings. Thin them if you want full-sized roots.

  • Parsnips

    Sow fresh seed. Roots can stay in the ground and are often sweeter after a frost. Mark the row before the tops die back.

  • Radishes

    A quick crop. Sow a short row every couple of weeks rather than one long row.

  • Lettuce

    Sow little and often. In hot weather it bolts, so give summer sowings some shade and water.

  • Spinach

    Spring and late-summer sowings are the usual windows. It bolts if it is hot and dry.

  • Kale

    Pick leaves a few at a time. Net the plants if pigeons are stripping them.

  • Summer cabbage

    This row is for summer and autumn cabbage, not the spring hearts sown the previous summer. Firm the soil when you plant.

  • Calabrese

    This is the green summer broccoli with one main head, not purple sprouting. Sow a short row more than once if you want heads over several weeks. Dry weather as the head forms makes it open and flower early.

  • Purple sprouting broccoli

    Sown in late spring and planted out in summer, it stands through winter and sprouts the following spring. It needs a long season. Pick the spears before the flowers open, and leave the plant to produce more.

  • Turnips

    A brassica, for rotation, even though you eat the root. Early sowings are for small roots. Later sowings can size up. Dry soil makes them woody. They dislike being transplanted, so sow them where they will grow.

  • Swede

    Also a brassica. Sow where it will crop. It wants a long, moist season or the root turns woody. Leave it in the ground and lift it as you need it. A hard frost improves the flavour for many people, but a very hard spell can damage the shoulder.

  • Brussels sprouts

    They need a long season and firm ground. Pick sprouts from the bottom of the stem upwards.

  • Leeks

    Transplant when the seedlings are about pencil thickness, into holes watered in rather than firmed hard.

  • Onions from seed

    Early indoor sowing needs a bright place or the seedlings stretch. Harvest when the tops fall over and the skins have dried.

  • Onion sets

    Spring sets are the straightforward option. Overwintering sets, planted in autumn, suit milder gardens and are left out of the northern months here.

  • Garlic

    Plant individual cloves, point up, in workable soil. Use cloves sold for planting. Autumn planting is the usual start.

  • Early potatoes

    Stand the tubers in a light, frost-free place to chit before planting. Cover the shoots if a late frost is forecast.

  • Maincrop potatoes

    Earth up the stems as they grow. Lift for storage once the tops have died back, on a dry day if you can.

  • Tomatoes

    Sow indoors and plant out only after the last frost. Outdoor plants need a warm, sheltered spot. Greenhouse plants can go out earlier into the house, not the garden.

  • Courgettes

    Two plants feed most households. They need sun, rich soil and regular water once they are cropping.

  • Cucumbers

    A greenhouse or cold frame is the usual home. Outdoor varieties still need a warm, sheltered spot.

  • Sweetcorn

    Plant in a block rather than one long row so the wind can pollinate it. It wants a warm summer.

  • Chillies

    Most seed-packet chillies are Capsicum annuum and need a long warm season. Sow on a bright windowsill. A pot in a greenhouse, porch or sunny room is the usual place they fruit. Outdoors, wait until the nights are warm and choose a sheltered spot.

  • Chard

    Cut outer leaves and leave the centre to grow on. It often stands into autumn.

  • Rocket

    A quick salad leaf. Sow a short row every few weeks rather than one long row. It runs to seed in hot, dry weather, so a summer sowing wants water and a little shade. Late sowings under cover keep leaves coming when outdoor plants have finished.

  • Spring onions

    Sow little and often. They do not need transplanting.

Herbs

  • Parsley

    Seed can be slow to germinate. A cloche or a pot by the door keeps a winter picking going longer.

  • Coriander

    It runs to seed in hot, dry weather. Sow a small patch more than once.

  • Basil

    Tender. A pot on a sunny windowsill is often more reliable than the open garden. Do not plant out until June.

  • Chives

    A clump comes back each year. Cut leaves as you need them and leave some to feed the plant.

  • Mint

    Plant it in a pot, or a pot sunk in the ground. In open soil it spreads further than you wanted.

  • Rosemary

    A small plant is the usual start. Seed is slow. It hates sitting in wet winter soil. Once established, pick lightly, including in winter.

  • Thyme

    Needs sun and drainage. Pick sprigs through the growing season. An established plant is evergreen, so a light winter pick is fine.
