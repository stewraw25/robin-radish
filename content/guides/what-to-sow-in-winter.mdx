Winter in a UK vegetable garden means December, January and February. The soil is cold, the days are short, and most seed will sit and rot if you treat the month like April. The useful jobs are a short list: garlic and other hardy plantings when the ground allows, a few pots under cover, and something green on the windowsill.

The month pages for [December](/what-to-sow/december), [January](/what-to-sow/january) and [February](/what-to-sow/february) list every crop in those months. [Garlic](/grow/garlic) and [broad beans](/grow/broad-beans) have their own notes, and [garlic and broad beans over winter](/guides/garlic-and-broad-beans-over-winter) is the longer one. A sketch for next year is in [planning a vegetable plot for 2027](/guides/planning-a-veg-plot-2027). Cloches, frames and the windowsill are in [sowing under cover](/guides/sowing-under-cover). The stretch after this, when the winter crops finish and summer ones are not ready, is the [hungry gap](/guides/hungry-gap).

If the soil is frozen or waterlogged, leave it. Walking on wet clay does more harm than a fortnight's delay. A plank to stand on is enough when you have to reach a row.

## Garlic and other things you plant, not sow

Garlic is the main winter planting. Use cloves sold for planting, not spare bulbs from the kitchen, and set each clove point upwards in workable soil. Autumn is the usual start. Planting can continue into the new year where the ground is still open. In colder gardens, aim to finish earlier and do not force cloves into frozen beds.

Onion sets for overwintering belong to autumn in milder areas. If that window has passed and you are in a cold or wet garden, wait and plant spring sets from March. There is a [sowing planner](/planner) if you want the month ranges for your half of the country.

Broad beans are the seed most people still sow in winter. Hardy types go into free-draining soil in a mild spell, or into pots in a cold frame or unheated greenhouse. In northern and inland gardens, pots under cover are the more reliable winter method. Outdoor sowing can wait until late winter or early spring.

## A bright windowsill

You can sow onion seed indoors from January, in a tray on a bright windowsill or in a greenhouse. The seedlings need light. A warm room with a dark sill produces long, weak plants. Move them somewhere cooler and brighter once they are up.

Pea shoots, cress and other thickly sown leaves are a separate idea. You are eating the shoots, not raising plants for the garden. A seed tray of compost, kept moist and cut when the growth is a few centimetres tall, gives a small picking in any month. Mustard, radish and peas are straightforward. Sow a new tray when you cut the old one.

That is not a substitute for a summer lettuce bed. It is a way to pick something green while the plot is quiet.

## What to leave alone

Do not sow tomatoes, courgettes, French beans, runner beans, cucumbers or sweetcorn in December or January. They need more light and warmth than a winter windowsill gives them, and you will be nursing leggy plants for months.

Tomatoes can wait until late February if you have a very bright, warm place and time to pot them on. March or April is a calmer start for most houses. The [sowing planner](/planner) keeps those crops in their usual months.

## Fruit, frames and the north

Winter is also the usual time to plant bare-root fruit trees and bushes, as long as the soil is not frozen or sodden. That is planting, not sowing, and it is worth doing while the plants are dormant.

Fleece, cloches and a cold frame protect seedlings you sowed in autumn. Open them on mild days so the air can move. Closed covers in damp weather turn into a mould box.

Southern and western gardens, especially near the coast, can sow hardy beans and plant garlic earlier than inland northern plots. The difference is often a few weeks, and a wet week matters more than the line on a map. If you are unsure, sow in a pot and keep it under cover until you can see growth.

Check anything you stored in autumn. One soft apple or a rotting potato will spoil its neighbours. Bring sound roots inside only if the store itself is freezing.

## Common questions

### Can I start tomatoes in January?

Leave them. January light is poor in most UK houses, and the plants will be drawn and pale before you can put them outside. Late February is early. March or April suits a normal windowsill.

### Is it too late for garlic in the new year?

Not if the soil is workable. Plant cloves through winter into February in many gardens, and into March further north if autumn passed you by. Do not plant into frozen or waterlogged ground.

### What can I grow indoors if I have no garden beds?

Pea shoots and other cut-and-come-again trays on a bright windowsill. A pot of parsley or chives by the door is also realistic. Treat it as a small picking, and keep the compost moist rather than sodden.
