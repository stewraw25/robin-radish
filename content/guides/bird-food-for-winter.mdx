From 1 November to 30 April, the RSPB says you can offer a full range of bird foods, including seeds and peanuts as well as mealworms, fat balls and suet. That is the other half of its "Feed Seasonally, Feed Safely" guidance, dated 30 October 2026. Natural food is scarcer in these months, and the RSPB says the risk from trichomonosis is lower, so the benefit of extra food outweighs the risk if you feed safely.

Feed safely means hanging feeders rather than a bird table or a flat tray, food kept dry, amounts the birds will finish while it is fresh, a weekly clean and a weekly move, and water only if you can change it every day. The RSPB's page is [Our latest guidance on feeding garden birds](https://www.rspb.org.uk/whats-happening/news/how-to-help-garden-birds). From 1 May to 31 October, pause seeds and peanuts. That change of season is in [feeding birds in spring and summer](/guides/feeding-birds-spring-summer). Cleaning is in [cleaning bird feeders](/guides/feeder-hygiene).

## What to put out from November to April

**Sunflower hearts** are the kernel with the shell removed. Tits, finches, sparrows and many other birds eat them. Husks do not pile up under the feeder. Put them in a hanging seed feeder. The RSPB asks that seeds are eaten within one to two days. Keep the bag dry.

**Nyjer** is a very small, oil-rich seed. Goldfinches and siskins take it. It needs a hanging feeder with tiny holes. In a normal seed feeder it pours onto the ground. Other birds usually ignore it, which is useful if pigeons empty everything else. Nyjer is still a seed, so it belongs in this November to April window, not through the summer.

**Suet** is sold as blocks, pellets or fat balls. Tits, starlings and woodpeckers take it in cold weather, and it can be offered all year. Hang it in a cage. The RSPB says suet can stay out for up to seven days. If a mild spell makes it soft enough to smear, take it down. Fat on feathers is a problem, not a help.

**Mealworms**, dried or live, are the food robins and blackbirds often take first, and they can be offered all year in small amounts. Soak dried mealworms in water before you put them out so they are easier to eat and hold a little moisture. A hanging mealworm feeder is a better place for them than a bird table. The RSPB asks that mealworms are eaten or cleared away by dusk the same day, especially if any are scattered on the ground.

**Mixed seed** is a blend, and the blend matters. A bag that is mostly wheat or maize is often kicked to the ground and can feed pigeons more than tits. Look for a mix with a clear share of sunflower and millet. Use a hanging seed feeder, and put out a day or two of food, not a heap that sits in the rain.

**Peanuts** belong in a rigid mesh hanging feeder so birds chip pieces off, rather than loose where a bird can take a whole nut. They are for 1 November to 30 April only, and should be eaten within one to two days.

**Apples and pears** on the ground are useful for blackbirds and for winter thrushes such as redwings and fieldfares. The RSPB lists bruised or unwanted fruit, so long as it is not mouldy. A windfall is a different thing from a feeder full of scraps. Dried fruit can go out in small amounts in a garden without cats or dogs, because raisins and sultanas are toxic to them.

## Water, amounts and squirrels

A shallow dish of water matters as much as seed when everything is frozen, but only put it out if you can change it every day. Break the ice in the morning. You do not need a deep bath. [Water for garden birds](/guides/water-for-garden-birds) is the longer note.

There is no fixed scoop that suits every garden. Put out what the birds finish while it is still fresh. Seeds and peanuts should not sit longer than a day or two. If food is still there at dusk and it has rained, you put out too much. Top up in the morning. Birds have a long night ahead of them.

Squirrels learn feeders. A baffle on a pole, a cage around the seed, or siting the feeder away from a jumping-off fence can slow them down. None of these stop every squirrel. Nyjer is less interesting to many of them than hearts or suet.

## What to leave out of the feeder

Do not put out salted, dry-roasted or flavoured nuts, mouldy seed, desiccated coconut, milk, or cooked food seasoned the way you would eat it.

Bread is not one of the foods the RSPB currently lists on its feeding guidance. It should not be used as a staple, and a few stale crumbs should not take the place of sunflower hearts, suet or mealworms.

Do not use a bird table or another flat feeder as the main offer. The RSPB's hygiene advice is to choose hanging feeders, because there is a higher risk of disease on a flat surface.

## Common questions

### Do I have to feed birds all year?

No. From 1 May to 31 October the RSPB asks you to pause seeds and peanuts. Small amounts of mealworms, fat balls or suet can still go out in a hanging feeder. Winter, from 1 November, is when the full range is useful again. Stopping for a holiday will not empty the garden of birds, as long as you empty the feeder before you leave and start again with clean equipment when you are back.

### Which food should I buy if I only want one?

From 1 November to 30 April, sunflower hearts in a hanging feeder suit the widest range of common garden birds. Add mealworms in a hanging feeder if a robin is the bird you most want to see. Add nyjer only if goldfinches visit and you have the right feeder. In May to October, choose mealworms or suet instead, not a bag of seed.

### Can I feed birds if I have a cat?

Yes, with some thought about where the food hangs. Give birds a clear view and a shrub to escape into, and do not put food in long grass beside a fence a cat can hide behind. A hanging feeder on a stand, a little way into the open, is easier to watch than a low tray.
