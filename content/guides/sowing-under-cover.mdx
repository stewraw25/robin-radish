Under cover means the seed is not in the open garden. That can be a windowsill, an unheated greenhouse, a cold frame, a cloche or a layer of fleece. The point is frost protection and a warmer start. It is not a reason to sow everything in January.

The [sowing planner](/planner) shows which crops have an indoor sowing at all. [Tomatoes](/grow/tomatoes), [courgettes](/grow/courgettes), [cucumbers](/grow/cucumbers), [leeks](/grow/leeks) and [onions from seed](/grow/onions) are the usual ones. Carrots, parsnips and beetroot are sown where they will grow.

Winter sowings are a short list. The [winter sowing](/guides/what-to-sow-in-winter) guide covers garlic, broad beans and the windowsill leaves. This page is the method.

## Where to sow

A bright windowsill is enough for a few pots. In January and February the light in the UK is poor. Seedlings sown too early, or kept warm and dark, stretch and fall over. For tomatoes, the planner months are the ones to use: later in the north than in the south. A week of weak plants on the sill is worse than a sowing made when the days are longer.

An unheated greenhouse or a cold frame is cooler than a living room and usually brighter. That suits leeks, onions, lettuce, broad beans and peas in pots. Tender crops still wait until the nights are not freezing the house.

A cloche or fleece over a row protects an outdoor sowing of lettuce, peas or broad beans. Fleece lets rain through. A plastic cloche does not, so you water underneath it. On a sunny day in March a closed cloche can cook seedlings. Open it.

## How to sow, and how to plant out

Use a potting compost and a clean pot. Sow thinly. Cover seed to about its own depth, except lettuce, which wants barely any cover. Water from below or with a fine rose, and tip out standing water.

If the seedlings are crowded, move them on before the roots are a solid block. Hold them by a leaf, not the stem.

Hardening off is the step people skip. A plant from a warm room will stall or die in a cold wind. Put it outside by day and bring it in at night for about a week, or stand it in a frame and open the lid a little more each day. Plant tomatoes and courgettes out only after the last frost you expect. In much of the UK that is late May, and later in the north and on high ground. The crop pages give the month ranges. They are ranges, not a forecast for your street.

If a late frost is forecast after you have planted, cover the row with fleece overnight and take it off in the morning.

## Trays and compost

You can search Amazon for [seed trays](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=seed+trays), [seed compost](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=seed+compost) or a [seed propagator](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=seed+propagator). ## Common questions

### Can I sow tomatoes in January?

Not usefully, on a windowsill. They sit for months in poor light and become weak. February and March suit southern and mild gardens. March and April suit the north. The [tomato](/grow/tomatoes) page has the split.

### What is damping off?

Seedlings collapse at the base and fall over. It is worse in crowded, wet, airless pots. Sow thinner, use clean pots, and do not leave them sitting in water. There is no need to start again with a different species. Sow a fresh pot.

### Is a plastic bottle a cloche?

A clear bottle with the base cut off will cover one plant. Remove the cap on mild days so the plant is not cooked, and peg the bottle down or it blows away. It is a small cover, not a greenhouse.
