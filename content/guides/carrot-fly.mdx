Carrot fly is a small fly whose larvae tunnel in the roots of [carrots](/grow/carrots) and sometimes [parsnips](/grow/parsnips). You usually notice it at harvest: rusty burrows in the root, and a smell. The adult is hard to see. It flies low, and it finds the crop by the smell of the foliage, which is strongest when you thin or bruise the leaves.

This page is about limiting the damage. It will not promise a clean row. A fine mesh cover, used properly, is the method that makes the biggest difference on a home plot.

## Cover the row

Use an insect-proof mesh, not a coarse bird net. The fly is small. Peg the edges down to the soil, or bury them, so there is no gap at ground level. A cover that flaps, or stops 10 cm short of the soil, is a door.

Put the cover on when you sow, or as soon as the seedlings show, and leave it on. Lift it to weed, then put it straight back. The [carrot](/grow/carrots) page says the same thing in shorter form.

A low barrier of about 60 cm can also work, because the fly travels close to the ground. It has to surround the bed. A single fence on the windward side does not.

## Thinning and smell

Thin in the evening, and take the thinnings away. Do not leave them on the bed. Water after you thin, which settles the soil and damps the smell a little. Sow thinly in the first place and you thin less.

Sowing later, from June, misses the first generation of flies in many gardens. It is not a guarantee, and it shortens the season. The sowing months on the [calendar](/sowing-calendar-2027) still run from March or April. A later sowing is a choice, not a correction of that list.

## What does not fix a tunnelled root

Once the larvae are in the root, no spray you can buy for a home garden will tidy it. Cut away the damaged part and eat the rest if it is sound. Compost the badly hit roots rather than leaving them to rot in the row.

Do not rely on a companion flower to “confuse” the fly. It is a popular idea. A sealed mesh cover is the thing you can actually check.

[Slugs](/guides/slugs-without-pellets) take seedlings. Carrot fly takes the root later. They are different problems and they need different answers.

## Mesh

You can [search Amazon for insect mesh](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=insect+mesh+garden). That is a search, not a particular brand. The cover has to be sealed at the soil and left in place, or the fly still finds the row.

## Common questions

### Are the rusty tunnels safe to eat around?

Cut out the tunnels. The rest of a firm root is fine to cook. A soft, rotting root belongs on the compost heap.

### Do parsnips get carrot fly too?

They can. The same low mesh cover is the practical protection. Parsnips stay in the ground into winter, so a cover that is still intact in autumn is worth leaving on.
