Winter is the usual time to prune many hardy fruit bushes and trees in a UK garden, while they are leafless and you can see the shape. The useful months are from late autumn until early spring, on a dry day that is not a hard frost. Frozen wood snaps and crushes. Wet wood is miserable to cut and the ground around the plant is easily compacted, so wait if the bed is a puddle.

This is a short, general note, not a shape for every tree. It does not name named systems or publish yields. If a plant is old, badly overgrown, or a trained cordon or fan you do not recognise, a specialist book or a local fruit group is a better next step than one afternoon with a saw.

A pair of sharp bypass secateurs will do for most stems. A lopper or a small pruning saw is for anything the secateurs cannot close on cleanly. Examples include [Felco 2 bypass secateurs](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B00G2H2N8M) and [Spear & Jackson telescopic bypass pruners](https://www.amazon.co.uk/dp/B0CTKL5VN5). Those are listings, not a ranking, and this page has no prices. The same tools appear on the [Christmas gifts](/guides/christmas-gifts-for-gardeners) page.

## What to prune in winter

**Blackcurrants.** These fruit best on newer wood. In winter, cut out a few of the oldest, darkest stems at the base, and leave the younger ones. You are renewing the bush over a few years, not trimming the tips.

**Redcurrants, whitecurrants and gooseberries.** These are usually grown as a short-legged bush with a framework of older branches. In winter, take out dead, crossing, or very low wood, and shorten the side shoots. The idea is an open centre that you can pick into, not a tight thicket.

**Summer-fruiting raspberries.** The canes that already fruited are brown and done. Cut those to the ground. Tie in this year's new green canes, which will fruit next summer. Do not cut the new canes down.

**Autumn-fruiting raspberries.** These fruit on this year's canes. In late winter you can cut all the canes to the ground, or leave a few of the strongest if you want a smaller earlier pick as well as the autumn crop. Do not treat them as summer raspberries, or you will cut off the wood that would have cropped.

**Apples and pears.** Winter is the usual time to take out dead, damaged, or crossing branches, and to shorten last year's growth if you are keeping a compact tree. Spur-bearing trees carry fruit on short stubs along the branches; do not strip those stubs off. Some varieties fruit at the tips of last year's shoots; if you shorten every tip, you will cut off the crop. If you do not know which you have, take out the obvious clutter this year and leave a close trim until you have watched it fruit.

**Bare-root planting.** Winter, when the soil is not frozen or sodden, is also the usual time to plant bare-root fruit. That is planting, not pruning, and it is mentioned in [what to sow in winter](/guides/what-to-sow-in-winter).

## What to leave

**Plums, cherries and other stone fruit.** These are usually pruned in summer, not in winter. A winter cut is more likely to let silver leaf disease in. If a branch has broken, cut it back to sound wood on a dry day and leave the rest of the shaping for summer.

**Spring-flowering shrubs**, and anything grown for blossom rather than fruit, should not be cut now if the flower buds are already on the wood. Leave them until after they flower.

**Newly planted bushes** need only damaged wood taken off in their first winter. Let them establish before you start a hard reshape.

[Netting soft fruit](/guides/netting-soft-fruit) is a later job, for when the fruit is ripening, and is about keeping birds off the crop without trapping them. It is not a winter prune.

## How to cut

Use a sharp tool and make a clean cut, just above a bud or back to a main stem, without leaving a torn stub. Do not paint the wound. Clear the cut wood if it is diseased; ordinary prunings can go on the compost if they are healthy.

Do not stand on wet soil to reach a far branch. A plank to stand on is enough. If the wood is frozen, stop.

## Common questions

### Can I prune on a frost morning if I wait until it thaws?

Better to wait for a day when the wood is not frozen at all. Partly thawed stems still crush. The bush will not mind a week's delay.

### I have a plum tree with crowded branches. Can I sort it out in January?

Leave it. A winter cut on a plum or cherry is the one to avoid. Wait until summer, after the fruit, and then take out a little at a time rather than opening the whole tree in one go.
