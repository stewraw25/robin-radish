A robin in a UK garden is usually one bird, or a pair in the breeding season. Robins hold a territory and drive other robins off. You cannot fill a small garden with them the way a feeder fills up with tits. What you can do is make the garden easier for the robin that is already nearby.

The species page is [robin](/birds/robin). Other birds that feed low are the [blackbird](/birds/blackbird) and the [dunnock](/birds/dunnock).

## Food, and where to put it

Robins feed low. They will sometimes learn a hanging feeder, but a ground tray or a bird table is the reliable offer. Mealworms are the food they take most readily. Soak dried mealworms before you put them out. Sunflower hearts and suet crumbs on the same tray are worth adding, especially in winter when insects are scarce.

The wider notes on those foods are in [bird food for winter](/guides/bird-food-for-winter), and the [quiz](/quiz) will weight mealworms more heavily if you tick robins.

Put the tray where the robin can see around it. A shrub or a climber within a short dash gives cover from a sparrowhawk and somewhere to retreat. Do not hide the tray in long grass or hard against a fence a cat can stalk along. A stand in a bit of open ground, with cover a metre or two away, is the usual compromise.

Clean the tray. Ground food goes off, and it picks up droppings. The [cleaning guide](/guides/feeder-hygiene) applies here even though the feeder is not a tube. Offer a little, often, rather than a pile that sits overnight.

A shallow dish of water nearby helps in a freeze and in a dry spell. Break ice in the morning.

## Cover, and the rest of the garden

A clipped lawn with no shrubs is a poor robin garden, however many mealworms you buy. Ivy, a mixed hedge, or a dense bush gives the bird somewhere to hunt and to hide. You do not need a wilderness. One or two shrubs and a patch of leaf litter under them are enough for a bird that picks insects from the ground.

Leave some leaf litter under the hedge through winter if you can bear it. Robins turn it over for food. That is compatible with a tidy vegetable bed a few metres away. Net the brassicas so pigeons, not the robin, are the birds you are excluding. Robins are not interested in your kale the way a pigeon is.

If you have a cat, the tray position matters more than the brand of food. Watch the first few days. If the cat waits under the tray, move the food.

## A nest box

Robins use an open-fronted nest box, not a small-hole box aimed at tits. Site it low, below about two metres, and hide it in ivy or a dense shrub rather than nailing it to a bare fence in full view. Face it away from the strongest sun and the wettest wind if you have a choice. A box on a wall that bakes in the afternoon is a poor site.

Put it up in autumn or winter so it is there before nesting starts. Once a bird is using it, leave it alone. Do not keep opening the box to look, and do not cut back the cover in April because the shrub looks untidy. You may get no nest. A box is an offer, not a booking.

Do not add a perch that gives a magpie a place to sit at the opening. Open-fronted boxes are already exposed. Hiding the box matters more than decorating it.

## What not to do

Do not chase a robin for a photograph, and do not feed it from your hand if that trains it to approach every person and every dog. A tray is enough.

Do not use mealworms as the only care you offer and then let the tray go mouldy. Fresh food, water, and a shrub beat a large bag of larvae on dirty wood.

Other birds will eat the mealworms too. Blackbirds in particular. That is not a failure. If you want the robin to get a share, a second small tray, a little further from where the blackbird lands, sometimes helps. Sometimes the blackbird still wins. The robin was eating insects in that garden before you arrived.

## Common questions

### Why has the robin stopped coming?

They move through a territory, they hide when they moult, and they vanish for a few days in hard weather if they have found food elsewhere. Check that the tray is clean, that a cat has not taken to sitting under it, and that the food is fresh. A gap is normal. Two robins fighting is also normal. The loser may leave.

### Will a nest box definitely be used?

No. Many boxes are never used. An open-fronted box, low and hidden, is the sort robins use when they use one at all. Dense shrubs are used as nest sites with no box. Leave those alone in spring as well.

### Can I feed a robin in a flat or a yard?

A tray on a balcony can work if birds already visit and cats cannot ambush it. Keep the amount small so you are not dropping food on the neighbours, and clean the tray. A window box shrub helps. If no birds come, food alone may not change that. The yard may simply be too enclosed.
