A robin in a UK garden is usually one bird, or a pair in the breeding season. Robins hold a territory and drive other robins off. You cannot fill a small garden with them the way a feeder fills up with tits. What you can do is make the garden easier for the robin that is already nearby.

The species page is [robin](/birds/robin). Other birds that feed low are the [blackbird](/birds/blackbird) and the [dunnock](/birds/dunnock).

## Food, and where to put it

Robins feed low and often take mealworms first. Soak dried mealworms before you put them out. Sunflower hearts and suet are worth adding from 1 November to 30 April, when insects are scarce. From 1 May to 31 October, follow the RSPB and pause seeds and peanuts; small amounts of mealworms, fat balls or suet can still go out. The seasonal detail is in [feeding birds in spring and summer](/guides/feeding-birds-spring-summer) and [bird food for winter](/guides/bird-food-for-winter). The [quiz](/quiz) will weight mealworms more heavily if you tick robins.

The RSPB's current "Feed Seasonally, Feed Safely" guidance, dated 30 October 2026, asks you to choose hanging feeders over flat feeders such as bird tables, because disease spreads more easily on a flat surface. A hanging mealworm or suet feeder is the better offer, even though robins will sometimes feed on the ground. If you still use a tray, keep the amount small, keep it dry, clean it every week and move it every week. The [cleaning guide](/guides/feeder-hygiene) applies here as it does to a tube.

The RSPB's page is [Our latest guidance on feeding garden birds](https://www.rspb.org.uk/whats-happening/news/how-to-help-garden-birds).

Hang the feeder, or place a tray if you use one, where the robin can see around it. A shrub or a climber within a short dash gives cover from a sparrowhawk and somewhere to retreat. Do not hide the food in long grass or hard against a fence a cat can stalk along. A stand in a bit of open ground, with cover a metre or two away, is the usual compromise.

A shallow dish of water nearby helps in a freeze and in a dry spell, but only put water out if you can change it every day. Break ice in the morning.

## Cover, and the rest of the garden

A clipped lawn with no shrubs is a poor robin garden, however many mealworms you buy. Ivy, a mixed hedge, or a dense bush gives the bird somewhere to hunt and to hide. You do not need a wilderness. One or two shrubs and a patch of leaf litter under them are enough for a bird that picks insects from the ground.

Leave some leaf litter under the hedge through winter if you can bear it. Robins turn it over for food. That is compatible with a tidy vegetable bed a few metres away. Net the brassicas so pigeons, not the robin, are the birds you are excluding. Robins are not interested in your kale the way a pigeon is.

If you have a cat, the feeder position matters more than the brand of food. Watch the first few days. If the cat waits underneath, move the food.

## A nest box

Robins use an open-fronted nest box, not a small-hole box aimed at tits. Site it low, below about two metres, and hide it in ivy or a dense shrub rather than nailing it to a bare fence in full view. Face it away from the strongest sun and the wettest wind if you have a choice. A box on a wall that bakes in the afternoon is a poor site.

Put it up in autumn or winter so it is there before nesting starts. Once a bird is using it, leave it alone. Do not keep opening the box to look, and do not cut back the cover in April because the shrub looks untidy. You may get no nest. A box is an offer, not a booking.

Do not add a perch that gives a magpie a place to sit at the opening. Open-fronted boxes are already exposed. Hiding the box matters more than decorating it.

## What not to do

Do not chase a robin for a photograph, and do not feed it from your hand if that trains it to approach every person and every dog. Food in a hanging feeder is enough.

Do not use mealworms as the only care you offer and then let the feeder go mouldy. Fresh food, water, and a shrub beat a large bag of larvae in a dirty dish.

Other birds will eat the mealworms too. Blackbirds in particular. That is not a failure. If you want the robin to get a share, a second small hanging feeder, a little further from where the blackbird lands, sometimes helps. Sometimes the blackbird still wins. The robin was eating insects in that garden before you arrived.

## Where to buy the food

[Haith's](https://haiths.com/) and [CJ Wildlife](https://www.birdfood.co.uk/) sell mealworms, sunflower hearts and suet. Buy a small amount of dried mealworms first and see if they are eaten. A large open bag goes stale. You can also [search Amazon for dried mealworms](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=dried+mealworms+bird+food), [suet cakes](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=suet+cakes+bird+food) or a [suet feeder](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=suet+bird+feeder). Those are searches, not a particular bag or feeder. Sunflower hearts belong in the 1 November to 30 April window, not through the summer.

## Common questions

### Why has the robin stopped coming?

They move through a territory, they hide when they moult, and they vanish for a few days in hard weather if they have found food elsewhere. Check that the feeder is clean, that a cat has not taken to sitting under it, and that the food is fresh. A gap is normal. Two robins fighting is also normal. The loser may leave.

### Will a nest box definitely be used?

No. Many boxes are never used. An open-fronted box, low and hidden, is the sort robins use when they use one at all. Dense shrubs are used as nest sites with no box. Leave those alone in spring as well.

### Can I feed a robin in a flat or a yard?

A hanging feeder on a balcony can work if birds already visit and cats cannot ambush it. Keep the amount small so you are not dropping food on the neighbours, and clean the feeder every week. A window box shrub helps. If no birds come, food alone may not change that. The yard may simply be too enclosed.
