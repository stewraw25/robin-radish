A vegetable patch can be one bed. It does not need to be a set of rectangles on day one, and it does not need to feed you for the year. The first job is a piece of ground you can reach with a hose, in enough sun to grow something you will actually pick.

When you are choosing crops, use the [sowing planner](/planner) or start with [lettuce](/grow/lettuce), [radishes](/grow/radishes) and [courgettes](/grow/courgettes). The [month pages](/what-to-sow) show the same list without the form. Once the bed is growing, [slugs](/guides/slugs-without-pellets), a [compost bin](/guides/compost-small-plot) and a simple [rotation](/guides/crop-rotation) matter more than another packet of seed.

## Light, water and size

Fruiting crops such as tomatoes, beans and courgettes want sun for a good part of the day in summer. Lettuce, chard, kale and mint will cope with a little more shade, though deep shade under a tree is a poor place for almost all vegetables.

Put the bed where you can water it. A water butt on a downpipe is worth plumbing in before summer, but a hose that reaches is enough to start.

Keep the first bed small enough that you will weed it. A single bed a couple of metres each way is plenty. You can add another next year if the first one gets looked after. Paths matter: if you walk on the soil you grow in, it packs hard, especially on clay. See [raised beds](/guides/raised-beds) if the ground is awful or you want a clear edge.

## Clearing the ground

Perennial weeds need to go before you sow. Bindweed, couch grass and docks will be back through a row of carrots if you only skim the tops. Digging the roots out works if you are thorough and you keep coming back for the pieces you missed. Covering the bed with something that blocks light, for a season, also works and disturbs the soil less. It is slower.

You can then dig, or you can mulch the surface and plant into pockets. Both are ordinary ways to run a UK plot. Digging helps if the soil is compacted and you are happy to do it. Do not work soil that is wet and sticky. It smears and sets like brick. Mulching with compost on the surface suits people who would rather not turn the ground every year. Neither method removes slugs.

Add garden compost in autumn or spring. You are feeding the soil, not chasing a perfect texture in week one.

## A simple first planting

You do not need a rotation chart before you own a spade. One useful habit: avoid putting cabbage, kale, sprouts and other brassicas in the exact same spot every year if you have room to move them.

Forgiving first crops, in a sunny enough bed:

- Potatoes, which you earth up and which open the ground as they grow
- Courgettes, if you have sun and you only plant one or two
- French beans, once the frost has gone
- Lettuce, radishes and spring onions, sown a little at a time
- Chard, which stands for a long picking
- Parsley, chives or mint in a pot

Use the [sowing planner](/planner) for months, and [what to sow in winter](/guides/what-to-sow-in-winter) if you are starting in the quiet season. Sow short rows of lettuce more than once. A single huge sowing all matures together.

## Slugs and the first month

Slugs are the usual reason seedlings vanish. Clear piles of weeds and old pots from beside the row, water in the morning rather than leaving the surface wet all night, and look at the plants at dusk. You can pick slugs off by hand. Traps and barriers help some gardens and do little in others. None of them clear slugs from a whole plot.

Net brassicas if pigeons find them. A net held off the leaves is enough. Birds are not the enemy of the rest of the garden: there is a separate set of notes on [feeding them](/guides/bird-food-for-winter), away from the seed bed so you are not training pigeons to scratch your sowings.

## Common questions

### Do I have to dig?

No. Digging and mulching both grow vegetables. If the soil is compacted, loosening it once is useful. If it is wet, leave it until it crumbles in your hand rather than smearing.

### What if I only have paving?

Use pots or a raised bed set on the paving, with drainage. Mint, salad, radishes, chard and a tomato in a large pot are realistic. Root crops need depth. Do not expect a courgette to be happy in a tiny pot.

### How soon can I sow after I clear the weeds?

When the soil is workable and the month matches the crop. Clearing weeds in November does not mean sowing carrots in November. Plant garlic if the season fits, or wait for spring and use the planner.
