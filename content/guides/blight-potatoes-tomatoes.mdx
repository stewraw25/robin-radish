Late blight is a disease of the potato family. On this site that means [early potatoes](/grow/early-potatoes), [maincrop potatoes](/grow/maincrop-potatoes) and [tomatoes](/grow/tomatoes). [Chillies](/grow/chillies) are in the same family for rotation, but the blight people mean in a wet August is the one on potatoes and outdoor tomatoes.

It shows up in warm, wet weather. You cannot time it to a date. Some summers it is early. Some summers a dry spell holds it off. A greenhouse tomato often escapes it because the leaves stay dry.

## What it looks like

On potatoes: brown patches on the leaves, sometimes with a pale edge, spreading quickly in a wet week. The haulm collapses. The tubers can develop brown rot under the skin if spores wash into the soil.

On tomatoes: brown patches on the leaves and stems, and fruit that turns brown and hard in patches, often starting at the shoulder. It is not the same as blossom end rot, which is a dry black patch at the flower end of the fruit and is about watering.

If you are unsure, look again after a wet day. Blight moves fast. A nutrient deficiency does not blacken a row overnight.

## What to do

Cut off and remove potato foliage as soon as the blight is obvious, and take it away. Do not compost it in a heap that does not get hot. Leave the tubers in the ground for a couple of weeks so any spores on the surface are less likely to touch them at harvest, then lift them on a dry day. Check stored potatoes and remove any that are soft.

On tomatoes, remove affected leaves and fruit. A plant under cover, with dry leaves, may carry on. An outdoor plant in a wet fortnight often does not. Pick any fruit that is still sound and ripen it indoors.

There is no tidy cure once a row is infected. Water the soil, not the leaves, so you are not keeping the foliage wet. Give outdoor tomatoes space and a stake so air can move.

## Next year

Do not plant potatoes or tomatoes in the same patch. That is the point of the [crop rotation](/guides/crop-rotation) note: tomato after potato is not a rotation. Clear any potato plants that come up from missed tubers. Those volunteers are a bridge for the disease.

Earlies are often lifted before blight arrives. That is a reason to grow some, not a promise. The months are on the [sowing calendar](/sowing-calendar-2027).

## Common questions

### Should I spray?

This site does not recommend a product. Removing infected foliage, keeping leaves dry, and not following potatoes with tomatoes will do more for a small garden than a bottle chosen from a label. If you do use a garden chemical, follow the label, and do not spray in a way that drifts onto the bird bath or the pond.

### Can I eat the potatoes?

Cut away any brown flesh. A firm tuber with no rot is fine to cook. Do not store damaged tubers with the sound ones. A rotten potato in a bag spoils the rest.
