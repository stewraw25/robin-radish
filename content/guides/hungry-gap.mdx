The hungry gap is the awkward stretch, often April into early June, when winter leeks, kale and stored roots are finishing and the summer crops are not ready. Courgettes, French beans and tomatoes are still small. The dates move with the winter you had. A mild south-west garden and a cold inland one do not run out on the same Monday.

This is not the same job as [what to sow in winter](/guides/what-to-sow-in-winter). That page is December to February. The gap is what you eat, and what you should have sown earlier, once spring is underway. The [April](/what-to-sow/april) and [May](/what-to-sow/may) pages list the sowings. The [planner](/planner) will narrow them to the south or the north.

## What may still be in the ground

[Kale](/grow/kale) and [leeks](/grow/leeks) are the crops most likely to be left. Pick kale leaves and leave the growing point. Lift leeks as you need them before they run to flower. [Chard](/grow/chard) sometimes stands through a mild winter and picks again in spring. [Parsley](/grow/parsley) and [chives](/grow/chives) start again before the beans do.

A row that has already bolted is not a crop. Spinach and lettuce that run to seed in the first warm week are finished. Do not wait for them to make a heart.

Purple sprouting broccoli is the vegetable people mean when they talk about food in this gap. It is sown in late spring for picking the following early spring. This site does not yet have a page for it. If you want that harvest next year, it has to be sown in this one.

## What to sow so the gap is shorter

Sow little rows of [radishes](/grow/radishes), [lettuce](/grow/lettuce) and [spring onions](/grow/spring-onions) as soon as the soil can be worked. [Spinach](/grow/spinach) sown in spring is a short crop before the heat. Under a cloche or on a bright sill these are earlier than in the open. The method is in [sowing under cover](/guides/sowing-under-cover).

The summer food that ends the gap is already decided by what you sow now: [courgettes](/grow/courgettes), [French beans](/grow/french-beans) and [tomatoes](/grow/tomatoes), started under cover and planted after frost. They will not feed you in April. They are why June is easier than May.

Stored onions, garlic and potatoes are part of the gap as well. When the sack is empty and the leeks are done, the garden really is between seasons. That is the point of sowing the quick crops, not a reason to strip the hedge for wild food you are not sure of.

## Common questions

### When exactly is the hungry gap?

There is no single week. In much of the UK it sits from about April until the first courgettes and early potatoes. A cold spring makes it longer. A garden that still has kale and leeks hardly notices the start of it.

### What can I sow in April that I will actually eat soon?

Radishes, lettuce and spring onions. They are the short crops. Everything else you sow in April is for summer. Check the [planner](/planner) for your half of the country before you fill a seed tray.

### Is the hungry gap a reason to buy plants?

You can plant a lettuce or a pot of herbs if the seed is late. It does not fill a bed. The gap is mostly solved the year before, by leeks, kale and, if you grow it, sprouting broccoli.
