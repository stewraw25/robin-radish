Slugs take [lettuce](/grow/lettuce), bean seedlings, [courgettes](/grow/courgettes) and young [brassicas](/grow/summer-cabbage) overnight. This page is the non-pellet side of the job. It does not rank products, and it does not claim a method that clears a wet UK spring.

Pellets are a separate choice. This note does not cover them. If you use them, that decision is yours and the label is the instruction, not this page.

## Make the bed less welcoming

Slugs hide in damp litter, long grass against the row, and the underside of boards and pots. Pull weeds from the edge of the seedbed. Water in the morning when you can, so the surface is not wet all evening. A seedling in a pot on a bench is easier to defend than the same plant at soil level.

Go out with a torch at dusk, especially after rain, and remove the slugs you find on the plants. A board or a grapefruit skin laid on the soil collects them by morning. Then you have to remove them. The trap does nothing if you leave the slugs there.

## Barriers, and the ones that disappoint

A clean copper band around a pot can put slugs off crossing the rim. It does not protect a row in the soil. Refresh it when it is green and dirty. A dry mineral barrier washes away in the first hard rain, which in Britain is often the night the slugs are busiest.

Crushed eggshells and coffee grounds are often suggested. They are not a reliable barrier. Do not depend on them for the only lettuce.

A beer trap drowns slugs. It also drowns ground beetles that eat slugs. If you use one, set it so the rim is a little above the soil, empty it, and do not treat it as the whole plan.

Biological controls that you water onto the soil need the soil to be mild and moist. They are pointless on a frozen bed, and they are a product with a label. This page is not a recommendation of a brand.

## What still needs a cover

Netting keeps pigeons off [kale](/grow/kale). It does not keep slugs off. Fleece and a cloche warm the row and can also make a damp shelter, so look underneath. [Sowing under cover](/guides/sowing-under-cover) gets plants to a size where one slug is less likely to finish them. A shredded seedling in a module has no second chance. Sow a few extra.

## Common questions

### Will a dry spring solve it?

It helps. Slugs do less on a dry, bare surface. One rainy night puts them back on the courgettes. Keep looking for a fortnight after you plant out.

### Are slug nematodes worth it on a small bed?

They are a way of watering a control onto moist, mild soil, not a substitute for picking slugs off a lettuce. Read the pack for the temperature and the area it covers. A cold March is too early.

### What should I protect first?

The plants that are still small: lettuce, basil, courgette and bean seedlings, and newly planted brassicas. A mature kale plant can lose a leaf. A two-leaf lettuce cannot.
