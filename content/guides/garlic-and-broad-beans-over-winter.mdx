Garlic and broad beans are the two crops on this site that are meant to sit through winter. They are not a reason to treat December like April. If the soil is frozen or waterlogged, wait. The month ranges below are the same ones as the [sowing planner](/planner) and the [crop pages](/grow).

The wider winter list is in [what to sow in winter](/guides/what-to-sow-in-winter). Fleece and last-frost timing are in [first frost dates](/guides/first-frost-dates). December and January have their own pages: [what to sow in December](/what-to-sow/december) and [what to sow in January](/what-to-sow/january).

## Garlic

Plant separate cloves sold for planting, point upwards. A bulb from the kitchen may be a type that does not suit a UK winter, and it can carry disease into the bed. Cover the clove with a couple of centimetres of soil. The [garlic](/grow/garlic) page has the spacing.

On this site the planting months are:

- South, meaning southern England, the Midlands and much of Wales: October, November, December, January and February, if the soil is workable.
- North, meaning northern England and Scotland: October, November, February and March. December and January are not on the northern list.

Autumn is the usual start. A later planting can still make a bulb, but a clove pushed into frozen or sodden ground is more likely to rot than to catch up. Wet winter soil kills more garlic than cold air does. If your bed holds water, use a raised row or a pot of free-draining compost, and do not bury the pot in a tray that stays full.

A light mulch is enough in a cold snap. Do not bury the shoot once it is up. Weed the row in a dry spell. Leave the bed alone when your boots smear the soil.

## Broad beans

Use a hardy type if you are sowing in autumn. The outdoor months on this site are:

- South: October, November, February, March and April.
- North: October, then March, April and May. November is not an outdoor sowing in the northern list.

Sowing in pots under cover is February and March in both regions, not December. A November pot is not a substitute for the northern outdoor month you have missed. If the ground is sodden in the south in November, wait for February rather than burying seed in mud.

Plants that went in during October or November should sit, short and sturdy, through the cold. They do not need feeding in December. Pigeons pull the tops. A net, pegged down so birds cannot walk under it, is the usual protection. Mice take seed before it sprouts. If a row disappears, sow again in the spring window rather than pouring more seed into the same frozen drill.

In spring, do not dig the plants out because they look small. They grow away once the days lengthen. A late hard frost may blacken the tips. The plant often shoots again from below the damage. Pinching the tops out later, once the first pods have set, is a separate job and is one way to reduce blackfly. It is not a winter task. See [aphids](/guides/aphids-without-sprays).

## What not to do in a freeze

Do not sow peas, carrots or anything tender because the garlic row has made the bed look active. Do not walk the bed just to look. Do not water a bed that is already wet. Snow on the foliage is not, by itself, a reason to fleece a hardy bean. Standing water is the problem to take seriously.

## Common questions

### Is January too late for garlic?

In the south, January is still on the planting list if the soil crumbles in your hand. In the north, January is not on the list. February is the next northern month. A clove planted into frozen ground in either region is better left in the packet.

### Can I sow broad beans in December?

Not in this list, outdoors or in pots. December is for plants that are already in, and for garlic in the south if you missed autumn. The next broad-bean sowing is February in the south, including under cover, and March outdoors in the north.

### The leaves have gone black. Have I lost them?

Often, no. A blackened tip after a hard frost can shoot again from lower down. A plant that has rotted at the stem, in waterlogged soil, will not. Pull a rotten one and wait for the next sowing month rather than replanting into the same wet hole.
