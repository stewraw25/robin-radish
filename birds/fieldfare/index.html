UK garden bird

Fieldfare

A large, noisy winter thrush with a grey head, chestnut back and a dark tail.

How to recognise one

About the size of a blackbird, often looking bigger because of the way it sits upright. Grey head and rump, chestnut-brown back, black tail, and a spotted breast with a yellow-buff wash. The call is a harsh chak-chak, often from a flock. A redwing is smaller, with a pale eye stripe and a rusty flank. A mistle thrush is also large, but it is paler and more evenly spotted, and it lacks the fieldfare’s grey head and black tail.

What to feed a fieldfare

Berries and fallen fruit, and worms and other invertebrates when the ground is soft. Halved apples on the lawn are the garden food they use. They often feed with redwings. They are not seed-feeder birds. Leave some hawthorn and holly until the fruit has gone, and do not put out cooked or salted scraps.

Nesting

Fieldfares are winter visitors in almost every UK garden. They very rarely nest in Britain, and a bird eating apples in December is not choosing a nest site. They do not use a nest box. The useful thing is fruit and an undisturbed lawn while the flock is there.

Common questions

What should I feed a fieldfare in the UK?

Berries and fallen fruit, and worms and other invertebrates when the ground is soft. Halved apples on the lawn are the garden food they use. They often feed with redwings. They are not seed-feeder birds. Leave some hawthorn and holly until the fruit has gone, and do not put out cooked or salted scraps.

Fieldfares are guarding the holly. Is that normal?

Yes. A flock, or sometimes one bird, will try to keep other birds off a berry tree. It lasts while the fruit lasts. You do not need to intervene.

What should I put out for fieldfares in a frost?

Apples on the ground. Break ice on a shallow dish of water as well. Seed and suet are for other species. Remove fruit that has gone mouldy.
