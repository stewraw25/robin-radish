UK garden bird

Coal tit

A small, grey-buff tit with a white patch on the back of the neck.

The drawing is a silhouette for shape only. Colours are in the text.

How to recognise one

Smaller than a great tit and about the size of a blue tit, without the blue tit’s yellow belly. Black cap and bib, white cheeks, and a white stripe on the nape that shows from behind. The back is grey and the underparts are buff. It often snatches a seed and flies off to hide it.

What to feed a coal tit

Sunflower hearts and suet. It is a regular on hanging feeders, especially where there are conifers nearby, and it caches food in bark and in the ground. Peanuts in a mesh feeder are taken too.

Try the bird food quiz if you want a short list of foods for the birds you actually see. Winter food, spring and summer and water sit alongside the species notes.

Nesting

A hole nester, often low, including holes in banks and stumps as well as nest boxes. A 25 mm hole is suitable. The nest is moss and hair. Leave established ivy and dead wood if you want those natural sites.

Buying food

Straight foods such as sunflower hearts, suet and mealworms. This is not a ranking of shops or of any bag.

Common questions

What should I feed a coal tit in the UK?

Sunflower hearts and suet. It is a regular on hanging feeders, especially where there are conifers nearby, and it caches food in bark and in the ground. Peanuts in a mesh feeder are taken too.

Why does a coal tit take one seed and leave?

It stores food. A seed hidden in bark or in the ground is how it deals with a busy feeder. That is ordinary behaviour, not a sign that the food is wrong.

Is a coal tit a sign of a particular garden?

They are widespread in the UK and are especially at home where there are conifers, but they also use ordinary broadleaf gardens and feeders.
