UK garden bird

Long-tailed tit

A tiny pink, black and white bird with a tail longer than its body.

How to recognise one

A small round body and a very long, narrow tail. Black and white, with a pinkish wash on the flanks. They travel in parties and call constantly, a thin high note. They are not shaped like a blue tit. The tail is the feature you see first.

What to feed a long-tailed tit

Insects in summer. In winter they come to suet, fat balls and sometimes sunflower hearts, often as a group that arrives and leaves together. A suet cage is the feeder they use. They rarely sit and eat a pile of mixed seed.

Try the bird food quiz if you want a short list of foods for the birds you actually see. Winter food, spring and summer and water sit alongside the species notes.

Nesting

A remarkable domed nest of moss, lichen and spider silk, usually in a fork of a shrub such as gorse, bramble or a garden bush. They do not use a standard hole-fronted tit box. Leave dense shrubs uncut through spring. Parties in winter often include young birds from more than one nest, and birds roost huddled together.

Buying food

Straight foods such as sunflower hearts, suet and mealworms. This is not a ranking of shops or of any bag.

Common questions

What should I feed a long-tailed tit in the UK?

Insects in summer. In winter they come to suet, fat balls and sometimes sunflower hearts, often as a group that arrives and leaves together. A suet cage is the feeder they use. They rarely sit and eat a pile of mixed seed.

Will long-tailed tits use a nest box?

Not a typical tit box. They build their own domed nest in a shrub. The useful thing in a garden is dense cover that is not trimmed in the nesting season.

What should I put out when a flock arrives?

Suet, in a cage, is the food they come for in cold weather. They may ignore a feeder of mixed grain. The flock often stays only a few minutes.
