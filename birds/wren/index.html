UK garden bird

Wren

A tiny brown bird with a cocked tail and a very loud song.

How to recognise one

One of the smallest birds in a UK garden. Warm brown, finely barred, with a thin beak and a short tail held upright. It creeps through hedges and woodpiles like a mouse. The song is a long, loud trill, surprising for the size of the bird. It is not a treecreeper, which is longer-billed and works up a trunk.

What to feed a wren

Insects and spiders. Wrens rarely eat seed, and they are not a reason to buy a particular mix. A messy corner of leaf litter, a log pile and a dense hedge feed them. If they visit a tray at all, it is usually for a few mealworms or crumbs of suet in hard weather. Do not expect them to become feeder birds.

Try the bird food quiz if you want a short list of foods for the birds you actually see. Winter food, spring and summer and water sit alongside the species notes.

Nesting

A domed nest of moss and leaves tucked into a hollow, a nest box used on its side, ivy, or a shed. The male may build several nests. The female lines the one she uses. Leave piles of brush and thick climbers through the spring.

Common questions

What should I feed a wren in the UK?

Insects and spiders. Wrens rarely eat seed, and they are not a reason to buy a particular mix. A messy corner of leaf litter, a log pile and a dense hedge feed them. If they visit a tray at all, it is usually for a few mealworms or crumbs of suet in hard weather. Do not expect them to become feeder birds.

What food brings wrens to a feeder?

Usually none. They hunt insects in cover. A garden with leaf litter and a hedge is more useful than a seed mix. In a hard freeze they may take a few soaked mealworms from a tray.

Something tiny is singing very loudly in the hedge. Is it a wren?

Very likely. The song is long and powerful for a bird that is barely the size of a ping-pong ball, and the tail is cocked when it pops out.
