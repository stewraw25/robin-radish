UK garden bird

Collared dove

A slim, pale dove with a thin black half-collar, common in gardens all year.

How to recognise one

Smaller and slimmer than a wood pigeon, with a longer-looking tail. Pale buff-grey all over, with a narrow black half-collar on the back of the neck, edged white. The tail has white corners that show when it is spread. There is no white neck patch and no broad white bar across the wing, which is how a wood pigeon looks. The song is a repeated three-note coo.

What to feed a collared dove

Seed and grain, mostly from the ground. They take spilled sunflower hearts and mixed seed under a feeder, and they will sit on a bird table. A hanging tube with short perches is a poor fit for them. If they are clearing the ground, put out less at a time so it is eaten while it is fresh. They also take buds and shoots. Net brassicas if pigeons of either kind are stripping them. Do not offer salted or dry-roasted nuts.

Nesting

A thin platform of twigs in a tree, a climber or on a ledge, used from spring into autumn and sometimes more than once. The nest can look unfinished. They do not use a tit box. A dove on the lawn in December is feeding, not nesting.

Buying food

Straight foods such as sunflower hearts, suet and mealworms. This is not a ranking of shops or of any bag.

Common questions

What should I feed a collared dove in the UK?

Seed and grain, mostly from the ground. They take spilled sunflower hearts and mixed seed under a feeder, and they will sit on a bird table. A hanging tube with short perches is a poor fit for them. If they are clearing the ground, put out less at a time so it is eaten while it is fresh. They also take buds and shoots. Net brassicas if pigeons of either kind are stripping them. Do not offer salted or dry-roasted nuts.

How do I tell a collared dove from a wood pigeon?

The collared dove is smaller, paler and slimmer, with a thin black half-collar. The wood pigeon is bulky and grey, with a white patch on the neck and a white bar on the wing.

Collared doves have eaten all the seed. What should I change?

Offer a smaller amount, more often, and use a caged feeder if you want the seed kept for tits and finches. Sweep up what is left at the end of the day. Nyjer is less useful to doves because the holes are too small for their bills.
