This week in the garden

16–22 November 2026

This week can still be mild, or it can freeze. Sow only where the soil crumbles in your hand. If it is frozen or sodden, harvest what is standing and keep water open for the birds.

Sow and plant

  • Broad beans, south only

    A hardy type can still go into free-draining soil in the south, ideally under a cloche. The northern outdoor list ended in October. Do not sow into mud in either region, and do not treat a November pot as the February indoor sowing.

  • Garlic

    November is a planting month in the south and the north, if you have not already done it and the soil is workable. Use cloves sold for planting, point upwards. Do not plant into frozen ground.

  • Hardy peas, south only

    In a mild southern garden, a hardy pea can go under a cloche or in a cold frame. The northern list does not include a November pea sowing. Elsewhere, leave the seed until the spring months on the planner.

  • Pea shoots on a windowsill

    A pot of pea seeds sown thickly for shoots is a kitchen picking, not a row in the bed. Cut them when they are a few centimetres tall. This does not replace the garden sowing months.

Harvest

  • Leeks

    Lift them as you need them. They stand through winter, so there is no need to clear the row.

  • Parsnips

    Leave roots in the ground and lift them after frost, when they are sweeter. Mark the row before the tops disappear.

  • Brussels sprouts and kale

    Pick sprouts from the bottom of the stem upwards. Take kale a few leaves at a time. Net the bed if pigeons are stripping it.

  • Swede

    Lift roots as you want them. A hard frost improves the flavour for many people. A very hard spell can damage the shoulder, so cover with a mulch if one is forecast and the soil is not already frozen solid.

  • Chard in the south

    November is still a southern harvest month for chard. Pick outer leaves while the plant is healthy. The northern list ends in October, and December is not a chard month in either region.

Birds to look for

  • Robins and blackbirds

    Both take soaked mealworms and sunflower hearts from a ground tray. A robin often feeds alone. Give it a clear view and a shrub nearby.

  • Tits

    Blue tits and great tits use hanging feeders. Sunflower hearts, and suet while the weather is cold, are the straightforward foods. Take suet down if a mild spell makes it soft enough to smear.

  • Finches

    Goldfinches take nyjer from a fine-mesh feeder. Bramblings, if they arrive, feed on the ground with chaffinches under the seed feeder.

  • Redwings and fieldfares

    These winter thrushes take berries and halved windfall apples on the lawn. They are not seed-feeder birds. Remove fruit once it rots.

What to put out

  • Sunflower hearts

    A husk-free seed that many garden birds eat. Put out what will be eaten before it turns damp. Wet November weather clumps a feeder that was filled too full.

  • Suet

    Blocks or pellets in a cage are a cold-weather food. Take them down if the week turns mild and the fat softens.

  • Nyjer seed

    For goldfinches and siskins. It needs a feeder with tiny holes, or it pours straight through.

  • Apples and mealworms

    Halved apples on the lawn for thrushes. Soak dried mealworms and offer them on a tray for robins and blackbirds.

Also this week

  • Break ice on a shallow dish of water in the morning. Do not add salt or antifreeze.
  • If seed has clumped or gone mouldy, empty the feeder, wash it, and refill with less.
  • Stay off wet soil. A plank is enough when you have to reach a row.
  • Sketch next year's beds while the plot is bare. The sowing months do not change because you planned them in November.
  • Check potatoes and apples in store and remove anything soft before it spreads.

Late November and early December

The note above is this week. These are the next ones, each on its own dates, for the turn into December.

  • 23–29 November 2026Late November jobs. Late November. The sowing list is nearly closed, frost is ordinary, and birds need food and water that are not left as ice or mould.
  • 30 November – 6 December 2026Into early December. This week runs from the last of November into early December. Little goes into the ground. Pick winter crops, and keep food and a water dish usable in a frost.

Common questions

Is Robin & Radish a shop?

No. It is a UK garden guide. Some links go to seed and bird-food shops. If an affiliate ID is set, the site may earn a commission when you buy. You do not pay extra.

Will the sowing months suit my garden?

They are general ranges for the north and south of the UK. Frost, soil and a cold spring all move the dates. Use them as a shortlist, then look at the weather you actually have.

Do I have to feed birds all year?

No. Winter is when natural food is harder to find and nights are long. If you do feed, keep the food fresh and the feeder clean.
