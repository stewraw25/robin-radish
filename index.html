This week in the garden

23–29 November 2026

Late November. The sowing list is nearly closed, frost is ordinary, and birds need food and water that are not left as ice or mould.

Sow and plant

  • Garlic, if the soil is open

    November is still a planting month in the south and the north. Set cloves sold for planting, point upwards, only where the soil crumbles. Frozen or waterlogged ground can wait. In the north the next planting month after November is February, not December.

  • Broad beans in the south, or not at all

    A hardy type can still go into free-draining southern soil, under a cloche if you have one. This is the last outdoor month for that sowing. The north is not sowing broad beans outdoors this week. Pots under cover start in February, not now.

  • Nothing tender

    Do not sow tomatoes, courgettes, French beans or cucumbers because the seed is in the house. A winter windowsill makes stretched plants. Those crops stay in their spring months.

  • Windowsill shoots

    Pea shoots, cress or a thick sowing of salad leaves in a tray are a kitchen crop. They are not a substitute for a bed, and they do not need the garden soil to be workable.

Harvest

  • Leeks and parsnips

    Take what you will eat and leave the rest. Mark parsnips before the foliage goes. Both crops stand in the ground in the south and the north.

  • Brussels sprouts and kale

    Pick sprouts upwards from the bottom of the stem. Take a few kale leaves, not the whole plant. Net if pigeons are on the row.

  • Swede

    Lift as needed. November is a harvest month in both regions. Cover the shoulder if a very hard frost is forecast and you can do it without walking on sodden soil.

  • Chard, south, while it lasts

    The southern harvest list includes November. Pick outer leaves if the plant is still sound. Do not expect a December picking from this list.

Birds to look for

  • Winter thrushes

    Redwings and fieldfares move between berry hedges and gardens. Halved apples on the lawn are the food they use. They ignore a tube of seed.

  • Blackcaps

    A grey warbler with a black or brown cap may take apple and suet. Winter birds in UK gardens are mostly visitors from continental Europe, not proof that a summer blackcap stayed.

  • Tits and finches

    Sunflower hearts and, in a cold spell, suet. Goldfinches need a nyjer feeder if that is the seed you bought. Bramblings feed on the ground with chaffinches.

  • Robins

    Soaked mealworms in a hanging feeder, with a clear view. The RSPB prefers hanging feeders to a bird table; if you still use a tray, keep it small, dry, and cleaned and moved each week.

What to put out

  • Smaller amounts

    Rain and frost both spoil a feeder that was filled for the week. Top up in the morning with what will be eaten.

  • Suet only while it is firm

    Cold weather is when suet earns a place. A mild day that softens it is when it comes down.

  • Water

    Break ice in the morning and again later if you are there. Pour a little warm water on the ice if you need to. Do not add salt, antifreeze or glycerine.

  • Apples

    Sound windfalls, halved, on the ground. Clear them when they collapse into a brown mush.

Also this week

  • Stay off wet beds. Planning on paper does more than a spade in the mud.
  • Wash a feeder that smells sour before you refill it.
  • Check stored potatoes, onions and apples and remove anything rotten.
  • Peg nets down on kale and sprouts if pigeons are getting underneath.
  • Order seed for crops you will actually eat. Match each packet to a month on the planner before spring.

Common questions

Is Robin & Radish a shop?

No. It is a UK garden guide. Some links go to seed and bird-food shops. If an affiliate ID is set, the site may earn a commission when you buy. You do not pay extra.

Will the sowing months suit my garden?

They are general ranges for the north and south of the UK. Frost, soil and a cold spring all move the dates. Use them as a shortlist, then look at the weather you actually have.

Do I have to feed birds all year?

No. Winter is when natural food is harder to find and nights are long. If you do feed, keep the food fresh and the feeder clean.
